## Post Content

Building an AI employee sounds cool until you realize the real challenge isn't the AI — it's the process.

I spent 6 months building an autonomous AI employee. It reads Gmail, processes WhatsApp messages, drafts replies, creates Odoo invoices, and posts to LinkedIn. 🤖 On paper, it works. In practice, I learned three lessons nobody tells you.

1. AI breaks at the seams, not the core.
The model is rarely the problem. The failures happen where AI touches real systems — unreliable APIs, inconsistent data formats, edge-case emails that break your prompt. Build for the seams, not the center.

2. Human approval isn't a bottleneck — it's a feature.
I almost removed the approval queue to make it "fully autonomous." That would have been a mistake. Every time I reviewed a pending task, I caught something. The approval step isn't overhead — it's trust-building. ✅

3. Logs are your best debugging tool.
When something breaks at 3 AM, you don't debug by re-running the AI. You read the structured log it wrote. Every action your AI takes should be timestamped and logged — treat logs as first-class output.

The lesson: building with AI is half engineering, half trust design. 🔧
