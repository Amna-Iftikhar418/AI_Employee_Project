## Post Content

The hardest part of building AI systems isn't the AI.

I spent six months building an autonomous assistant that reads email, drafts replies, and posts updates. The model was the easy part. Everything around it broke.

Three lessons that cost me the most:

1. Reliability beats intelligence. A "smart" system that fails 1 in 10 times is worse than a dumber one that never surprises you. I added a human approval gate before every external action — and trust went up overnight. 🛡️

2. Logs are a feature, not an afterthought. When something broke at 2am, I didn't need a smarter model. I needed to know what it actually did. Every action now writes a timestamped trail.

3. Constraints make agents useful. The moment I capped what it could do without approval, it became something I'd actually deploy.

The pattern underneath all three: treat the AI as a junior employee, not a magic box. Give it clear rules, a paper trail, and a manager who signs off.

Capability is cheap now. Trust is what you're actually building. 🔑

If you've shipped an AI agent to real users — what broke first for you?
