## Proposed Post

No-code tools promised to replace developers. They didn't — but they changed everything else.

I watched a small e-commerce business owner build a full order management system last year.
No developer. No budget. Just Zapier, Airtable, and a lot of patience.

It worked. Until it didn't.

When they needed a custom discount rule, the no-code platform had a hard ceiling.
When their data grew to 50,000 rows, Airtable slowed to a crawl.
When they tried to connect two tools that "should" integrate, the API broke every week.

No-code tools are not replacements for software. They're accelerators.

Here's the rule the best operators follow:
Use no-code to validate the idea.
Use code to scale the solution.

The mistake most businesses make is treating these as either-or decisions. 🛠

The real future of no-code isn't eliminating engineers — it's letting non-engineers become builders. That shift changes who gets to solve problems. 🚀

What's the biggest wall you've hit with a no-code tool?
