## Proposed Post

No-code tools didn't democratize software — they created a new ceiling.

The promise was that anyone could build apps without writing a single line of code. And technically, that's true. Zapier connects your Gmail to Notion. Bubble lets you drag a database into a UI. Glide turns a spreadsheet into a mobile app.

But here's what happened in practice:

1. Small businesses hit the limits of drag-and-drop within 6 months
2. The "no-code" work became a full-time job for someone who still didn't understand the underlying system
3. When things broke — and they always break — no one knew why

The companies that actually scaled with no-code weren't using it to replace developers. They were using it to prototype fast, validate assumptions, and then hand off to engineers who could build the real thing. 🔁

That's the future of no-code: not a replacement for code, but a translation layer between the idea and the investment.

The new paradigm is "low-code first, then code where it matters." The tools that survive the next wave will be the ones that let you export real code when you've outgrown the template. 🛠️

What's the biggest limitation you've run into with no-code tools?
